Tail Immersion Test

The distal ~5 cm of a rat's tail is immersed in a water bath held at 55 degrees C, and the latency to withdraw the tail is recorded. It shares features of both the tail-flick and hot-plate assays and is especially useful for repeated within-animal dose-response testing, since immersion is less tissue-damaging than radiant heat. Opioids prolong latency dose-dependently; a 15 s cut-off is enforced.
